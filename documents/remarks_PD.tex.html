 54 \begin{abstract}
 55 Adiprasito, Huh, and Katz show Poincar\'e duality of matroids using matroidal flips, which are purely combinatorial notions.
 56 Later, Hsiao, Karu, and Yang investigate the Hodge theory of matroids via star subdivisions, which correspond to blow-ups of toric varieties.
 57 This is an expository paper reviewing a simple proof of Poincar\'e duality of matroids using their method of star subdivisions and Keel's lemma from algebraic geometry.
 58 \end{abstract}
 59 \maketitle
 60 \section{Introduction}
 61 Let \(n, r\) be non-negative integers.
 62 In this paper, a \emph{matroid} \(M\) refers to a loopless matroid of rank \(r+1\) on an \((n+1)\)-set \(E\).
 63 Write \(\symcal{L}(M)\) for the lattice of flats of \(M\).
 64 Let \(\bigoplus_{i \in E}\symbf{Z}e_i\) be the free abelian group with \(E\) as its basis.
 65 Put \(e_S \coloneq\sum_{i \in S}e_i\) for a subset \(S\subset E\), \(N_E\coloneq \bigl(\bigoplus_{i\in E}\symbf{Z}e_i\bigr)/\symbf{Z}e_E\), and \(N_{E,\symbf{R}}\coloneq N_E\otimes_{\symbf{Z}}\symbf{R}\).
 66 The \emph{Bergman fan} \(\Sigma_M\) of a matroid \(M\) is the pure \(r\)-dimensional unimodular fan
 67 \begin{equation}
 68 \big\{\,\Cone (e_F:F \in\symcal{F}) : \text{\(\symcal{F}\subset \symcal{P}(M)\) is a flag}\,\big\}
 69 \end{equation}
 70 in \(N_{E,\symbf{R}}\).
 71 Here, \(\symcal{P}(M)\coloneq \symcal{L}(M)\setminus \{\emptyset,E\}\), and \(\symcal{F} \subset \symcal{P}(M)\) is called a \emph{flag} if it is linearly ordered with respect to the inclusion relation.
 72 Associated to \(\Sigma_M\), we can consider the \(n\)-dimensional smooth quasi-projective complex toric variety \(X_{\Sigma_M}\), which is possibly non-compact.
 73 Then, the \emph{Chow ring} \(A^{\bullet}(M)\) of \(M\) is defined to be the Chow ring \(A^{\bullet}\raisebox{-.25ex}{\big(}X_{\Sigma_M}\raisebox{-.25ex}{\big)}\) of \(X_{\Sigma_M}\).
 74 Adiprasito, Huh, and Katz show Poincar\'e duality of \(M\) in the following sense.
 75 \begin{theorem}[{\cite[Theorem 6.19]{AHK_18}}]\label{thm_2}
 76 For a matroid \(M\) of rank \(r+1\), \(A^{\bullet}(M)\) satisfies Poincar\'e duality of dimension \(r\), i.e., it satisfies
 77 \begin{enumerate}[label=$\arabic*)$]
 78 \item there exists a group isomorphism \(\deg\colon A^r(M)\similarrightarrow \symbf{Z}\), called a \emph{degree map},
 79 \item for every integer \(q > r\), we have \(A^q(M) = 0\), and
 80 \item for every integer \(q \leqq r\), the map
 81 \begin{equation}
 82 A^{r-q}(M) \to \Hom(A^q(M),\symbf{Z});\quad x \mapsto \big(y \mapsto\deg(xy)\big)
 83 \end{equation}
 84 is a group isomorphism, i.e., the multiplication \(A^q(M)\times A^{r-q}(M) \to A^r(M)\similarrightarrow \symbf{Z}\) is a perfect pairing.
 85 \end{enumerate}
 86 \end{theorem}
 87 The key concepts of their proof are matroidal flips, which are purely combinatorial technical notions.
 88 On the other hand, motivated by \cite{BHMPBW_22}, Hsiao, Karu, and Yang \cite{HKY_26} calculate mixed volumes of matroids using the deletion of matroids.
 89 For their computation, they utilize the following important fact.
 90 For a non-coloop \(a\in E\) of \(M\) and the deletion \(M\backslash a\), the projection \(\pi\colon N_E \to N_{E\setminus\{a\}}\) naturally induces a morphism of fans \(\pi\colon \Sigma_M \to \Sigma_{M\backslash a}\), and this morphism factors as
 91 \begin{equation}\label{eq_1}\tag{$\clubsuit$}
 92 \begin{tikzcd}[column sep =3em]
 93 \Sigma_M = \Sigma_{m} \arrow[r,"\pi_{m}"] & \Sigma_{m-1} \arrow[r,"\pi_{m-1}"]& \cdots \arrow[r,"\pi_{2}"]& \Sigma_{1} \arrow[r,"\pi_{1}"]& \Sigma_0 \arrow[r,"\pi_0"]& \Sigma_{M\backslash a},
 94 \end{tikzcd}
 95 \end{equation}
 96 where the morphism \(\pi_0\colon \Sigma_0\to \Sigma_{M\backslash a}\) is induced by the projection \(\pi\colon N_E \to N_{E\setminus\{a\}}\), \(\pi_i\colon \Sigma_{i} \to \Sigma_{i-1}\) is induced by the identity map, and \(\Sigma_i\) is a star subdivision of \(\Sigma_{i-1}\) at a 2-dimensional unimodular cone for each \(i=1,\dotsc,m\).
 97 Using the factorization \eqref{eq_1}, Hsiao \cite{Hsiao_25} gives a simpler combinatorial proof that \(A^{\bullet}(M)\otimes_{\symbf{Z}}\symbf{R}\) satisfies the hard Lefschetz property, the Hodge-Riemann relations, and therefore Poincar\'e duality.
 98 We note that Amini and Piquerez \cite[Section 1.9]{AP_23} also sketched a similar proof of these properties for matroids via star subdivisions, relying on the weak factorization theorem for tropical fans.
 99 In \S \ref{sec_1}, we will give a proof of Theorem \ref{thm_2} in the language of algebraic geometry using \eqref{eq_1} and Keel's lemma.

101 \section{Keel's lemma}\label{sec_2}
102 In this section, we recall an algebro-geometric lemma, which is known as Keel's lemma.
103 \begin{theorem}[Keel's lemma, cf.\ {\cite[Appendix, Theorem 1]{Keel_92}}]\label{thm_1}
104 Let \(X\) be a smooth variety, \(i\colon Z\hookrightarrow X\) a smooth closed subvariety of codimension \(z\), \(\pi\colon \widetilde{X}\to X\) the blow-up of \(X\) along \(Z\), and \(E\) the exceptional divisor, that is, we consider the pullback diagram
105 \begin{equation}
106 \begin{tikzcd}
107 E \arrow[r,hook]\arrow[d]\arrow[rd,phantom,"\pullback",very near start]& \widetilde{X}\arrow[d,"\pi"]\\
108 Z\arrow[r,hook,"i"'] & X.
109 \end{tikzcd}
110 \end{equation}
111 If the pullback map \(i^*\colon A^{\bullet}(X) \to A^{\bullet}(Z)\) is surjective, then the map \(\pi^*\colon A^{\bullet}(X)[T]\to A^{\bullet}(\widetilde{X});\ T\mapsto -[E]\) induces an isomorphism
112 \begin{equation}
113 \begin{tikzcd}
114 \displaystyle \frac{A^{\bullet}(X)[T]}{(P(T), T\cdot \Ker i^*)} \arrow[r,"\text{\large$\sim$}" yshift=-.3ex]& A^{\bullet}(\widetilde{X})
115 \end{tikzcd}
116 \end{equation}
117 where \(P(T) \in A^{\bullet}(X)[T]\) is a monic polynomial of degree \(z\).
118 In particular, we have a decomposition
119 \begin{equation}\label{eq_2}\tag{$\diamondsuit$}
120 A^q(\widetilde{X}) = A^q(X)\oplus [E]\cdot A^{q-1}(Z) \oplus\dotsb\oplus [E]^{z-1}\cdot A^{q-z+1}(Z)
121 \end{equation}
122 for all \(q\), where we embed \(\pi^*\colon A^{\bullet}(X) \hookrightarrow A^{\bullet}(\widetilde{X})\), and for all positive integers \(p,q\),
123 \begin{equation}
124 \begin{tikzcd}[column sep = 4em]
125 A^{q-p}(Z)\arrow[r,"(i^*)^{-1}"',"\text{\large$\sim$}" yshift=-.3ex]& A^{q-p}(X)/\Ker i^* \arrow[r,hook,"{[E]^p\cdot(-)}"']& A^{q}(\widetilde{X}).
126 \end{tikzcd}
127 \end{equation}
128 \end{theorem}
129 Now we go back to our toric case.
130 Let \(\Sigma\) be a unimodular fan in \(N_{E,\symbf{R}}\).
131 For a \(z\)-dimensional cone \(\sigma \in \Sigma\), let \(\Sigma_{\sigma}\) denote the star fan of \(\Sigma\) at \(\sigma\), and let \(\Sigma^*(\sigma)\) denote the star subdivision of \(\Sigma\) at \(\sigma\).
132 The orbit closure \(V(\sigma)\) associated to \(\sigma\) is the smooth toric subvariety \(X_{\Sigma_{\sigma}}\) of codimension \(z\), and \(\pi\colon X_{\Sigma^*(\sigma)}\to X_{\Sigma}\) is the blow-up of \(X_{\Sigma}\) along \(V(\sigma)\).
133 Since the ring \(A^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma})\) is generated by the classes of torus-invariant divisors, the pullback homomorphism \(A^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma}) \to A^{\bullet}\big(X_{\Sigma_{\sigma}}\big)\) is always surjective, so the assumption of Theorem \ref{thm_1} is satisfied if we substitute \(X=X_{\Sigma}\), \(Z=V(\sigma)=X_{\Sigma_{\sigma}}\), \(\widetilde{X}=X_{\Sigma^*(\sigma)}\).
134 We also note that the \(q\)-th degree part \(A^q(X_{\Sigma})\) is a finitely generated abelian group.
135 \begin{corollary}\label{cor_1}
136 We further assume that \(A^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma})\) and \(A^{\bullet}\big(X_{\Sigma_{\sigma}}\big)\) satisfy Poincar\'e duality of dimension \(r\) and \(r-z\) respectively.
137 Then \(A^{\bullet}\big(X_{\Sigma^*(\sigma)}\big)\) satisfies Poincar\'e duality of dimension \(r\).
138 \end{corollary}
139 \begin{proof}
140 The decomposition \eqref{eq_2} tells us that \(A^r\big(X_{\Sigma^*(\sigma)}\big)\cong \symbf{Z}\) and \(A^q\big(X_{\Sigma^*(\sigma)}\big)=0\) for each \(q>r\).
141 By Poincar\'e duality of \(A^{\bullet}(X)\) and \(A^{\bullet}(Z)\), each summand of \eqref{eq_2} is a finitely generated free abelian group.
142 With an apropriately ordered basis chosen from each summand, the representation matrix of the bilinear pairing \(A^q\big(X_{\Sigma^*(\sigma)}\big)\times A^{r-q}\big(X_{\Sigma^*(\sigma)}\big)\to \symbf{Z}\) is a block lower triangular matrix
143 \begin{equation}
144 \begin{bmatrix}
145 P_0 & O & \cdots &\cdots &O \\
146 O& P_1& \ddots& \ddots& \vdots \\
147 \vdots& \ast& \ddots & \ddots & \vdots\\
148 \vdots& \vdots& \ddots & \ddots & O\\
149 O&\ast&\ast&\ast&P_{z-1}
150 \end{bmatrix}.
151 \end{equation}
152 Poincar\'e duality of \(A^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma})\) and \(A^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma_{\sigma}})\) imply that \(\det P_q = \pm 1\) for all \(q=0,1,\dotsc,z-1\), and therefore the above representation matrix has determinant \(\pm 1\).
153 \end{proof}

156 \section{A proof of Poincar\'e duality by star subdivisions}\label{sec_1}
157 In this section, we prove Theorem \ref{thm_2} by induction on \(n\).  Before starting the proof, we recall the definition of a coloop.
158 \begin{definition}
159 An element \(a\in E\) is called a \emph{coloop} of \(M\) if the subset \(E\setminus\{a\}\) is a flat of \(M\), or equivalently, \(\dim \Sigma_{M\backslash a}< \dim \Sigma_M\).
160 \end{definition}
161 If all elements of \(E\) are coloops of \(M\), then \(M\) must be the Boolean matroid on \(E\).
162 The toric variety \(X_{\Sigma_M}\) is a smooth projective complex variety of dimension \(r=n\).
163 By the Jurkiewicz-Danilov theorem, the cycle map \(A^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma_M})\to H^{2\bullet}(X_{\Sigma_M},\symbf{Z})\) is an isomorphism, and thus Poincar\'e duality of \(A^{\bullet}(M)\) follows in the usual sense.
164 In particular, this establishes the statement for \(n=0\).
165 
166 We assume that \(n>0\) and there exists a non-coloop \(a\in E\) of \(M\).
167 If the singleton \(\{a\}\) is not a flat of \(M\), then the deletion map \(d\colon \symcal{L}(M) \to \symcal{L}(M\backslash a);\ F \mapsto F\setminus\{a\}\) is bijective, and \(\Sigma_M \subset (e_a-e_b)^{\perp}\) for some element \(b\) parallel to \(a\).
168 This implies that  \(X_{\Sigma_M}\cong X_{\Sigma_{M\backslash a}}\times \symbf{G}_{\symrm{m}}\), and hence \(A^{\bullet}(M)\cong A^{\bullet}(M\backslash a)\), where the right-hand side satisfies Poincar\'e duality of dimension \(r\) by the inductive hypothesis.
169 Hence we may assume that \(\{a\}\) is a flat of \(M\).
170 Now we construct the factorization \eqref{eq_1}.
171 Logically, the set \(\symcal{P}(M)\) can be partitioned into four disjoint subsets
172 \begin{align}
173 \symcal{P}(M) &=\big\{\,F:a \not\in F,\ F \cup\{a\}\in\symcal{P}(M)\,\big\}\sqcup\big\{\,F: a\not\in F,\ F \cup \{a\} \not\in \symcal{P}(M)\,\big\}\\
174 &\hspace{11em}\sqcup\big\{\,F: a\in F,\ F \setminus\{a\} \in \symcal{P}(M)\,\big\}\sqcup\big\{\,F: a\in F,\ F \setminus \{a\} \not\in \symcal{P}(M)\,\big\}\\
175 &\eqcolon \symcal{P}_1\sqcup\symcal{P}_2\sqcup\symcal{P}_3\sqcup\symcal{P}_4.
176 \end{align}
177 Under the deletion map \(d\colon \symcal{P}(M)\to \symcal{P}(M\backslash a)\), we have
178 \[
179 \symcal{P}(M\backslash a)=\symcal{P}_1\sqcup \symcal{P}_2 \sqcup d(\symcal{P}_4),\qquad d(\symcal{P}_3)\subset \symcal{P}_1,\qquad \symcal{P}(M/a)=d(\symcal{P}_3)\sqcup d(\symcal{P}_4).
180 \]
181 Consider the inverse map \(d^{-1}\colon \symcal{P}(M\backslash a)\to \symcal{P}(M)\setminus \symcal{P}_3\).
182 We construct a fan \(\Sigma_0\) in \(N_{E,\symbf{R}}\) by
183 \begin{align}
184 &\Sigma_0\coloneq \big\{\,\Cone(e_{d^{-1}(F)}:F \in \symcal{F}): \symcal{F}\underset{\text{flag}}{\subset}\symcal{P}(M\backslash a)\,\big\}\\
185 &\hspace{12em}\cup \big\{\,\Cone(e_{d^{-1}(F)}:F \in \symcal{F})+\Cone (e_a):\symcal{F}\underset{\text{flag}}{\subset} \symcal{P}(M/a)\,\big\}
186 \end{align}
187 (the latter cones are intended to recover the rays associated to \(\symcal{P}_3\)).
188 Observing that \(\Sigma_0\) has exactly one more ray than \(\Sigma_{M\backslash a}\) \big(the ray is \(\Cone(e_a)\)\big), one can show that the projection \(\pi_0\colon\Sigma_0 \to \Sigma_{M\backslash a}\) induces an isomorphism \(\pi_0^*\colon A^{\bullet}(M\backslash a) \similarrightarrow A^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma_0})\).
189 We order the flats \(d(\symcal{P}_3)=\{F_1, F_2,\dotsc, F_m\}\) so that \(F_i\subset F_j\) implies that \(i \leqq j\).
190 Recall that, since \(d(\symcal{P}_3)\subset \symcal{P}_1\), we have \(d^{-1}(F_i)=F_i\) for all \(i\), and hence \(\Cone(e_a,e_{F_i})\in \Sigma_0\) for all \(i\).
191 Let \(\Sigma_1\) be the star subdivision of \(\Sigma_0\) at \(\Cone(e_{F_m},e_a)\).
192 \(\Sigma_1\) has an extra ray \(\Cone(e_{F_{m}\cup\{a\}})\), and all new cones arising from this subdivision are of the form
193 \begin{align}
194 \Cone (e_{d^{-1}(F)}: F \in \symcal{F})+\Cone (e_{F_m\cup\{a\}}) &\quad \text{where \(\symcal{F}\) is a flag of \(\symcal{P}(M/a)\), or}\\
195 \Cone (e_{d^{-1}(F)}: F \in \symcal{F})+\Cone(e_a)+\Cone (e_{F_m\cup\{a\}}) &\quad \text{where \(\symcal{F}\) is a flag of \(\symcal{P}(M/a)\setminus\{F_m\}\).}
196 \end{align}
197 The cones of the former form are already cones of \(\Sigma_M\).
198 By recursively constructing the star subdivision \(\Sigma_{i+1}\) of \(\Sigma_i\) at \(\Cone(e_{F_{m-i}},e_a)\) for each \(i=1,\dotsc,m-1\), we obtain \(\Sigma_{m}=\Sigma_M\) and the desired factorization
199 \begin{equation}\tag{$\clubsuit$}
200 \begin{tikzcd}[column sep =3em]
201 \Sigma_M = \Sigma_{m} \arrow[r,"\pi_{m}"] & \Sigma_{m-1} \arrow[r,"\pi_{m-1}"]& \cdots \arrow[r,"\pi_{2}"]& \Sigma_{1} \arrow[r,"\pi_{1}"]& \Sigma_0 \arrow[r,"\pi_0"]& \Sigma_{M\backslash a}.
202 \end{tikzcd}
203 \end{equation}
204 
205 Finally, the star fan of \(\Sigma_i\) at \(\Cone(e_{F_{m-i}},e_a)\) is isomorphic to the product \(\Sigma_{[\emptyset,F_{m-i}]}\times \Sigma_{[F_{m-i}\cup\{a\},E]}\), where \([\emptyset,F_{m-i}]\) denotes the restriction of \(M\) to \(F_{m-i}\) and \([F_{m-i}\cup\{a\},E]\) denotes the contraction of \(M\) by \(F_{m-i}\cup\{a\}\).
206 This implies that its Chow ring is isomorphic to \(A^{\bullet}([\emptyset,F_{m-i}])\otimes A^{\bullet}([F_{m-i}\cup\{a\},E])\), which satisfies Poincar\'e duality of dimension \((\rk_M(F_{m-i})-1)+(\crk_M(F_{m-i}\cup\{a\})-1)=r-2\) by the inductive hypothesis.
207 Applying Corollary \ref{cor_1} with \(z=2\), we conclude that \(A^{\bullet}(M)\) satisfies Poincar\'e duality of dimension \(r\).
208 \qed

212 We give some remarks of the proof.
213 \begin{remark}
214 If we subdivide \(\Sigma_0\) at \(\Cone(e_{F_1},e_a)\), the resultant fan differs from \(\Sigma_M\).
215 For instance, when \(F_1\subset F_2\), \(\Cone(e_{F_1},e_{F_2},e_a)\) is divided into \(\Cone(e_{F_1\cup\{a\}},e_{F_2},e_a)\) and \(\Cone(e_{F_1\cup\{a\}},e_{F_1},e_{F_2})\).
216 The former has a face \(\Cone(e_{F_1\cup\{a\}},e_{F_2})\), which remains in the final fan and is not a cone of Bergman fan.
217 \end{remark}
218 \begin{remark}
219 In the language of tropical geometry, the fan \(\Sigma_0\) is known as the tropical modification of the tropical fan \(\Sigma_{M\backslash a}\) along the tropical divisor \(\Sigma_{M/a}\) with respect to a suitable piecewise linear function on \(\Sigma_{M\backslash a}\).
220 Then the isomorphism \(\pi^*\colon A^{\bullet}(M\backslash a)\to A^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma_0})\) follows from \cite[Theorem 6.4]{AP_23}.
221 \end{remark}
222 \begin{remark}
223 Amini and Piquerez \cite[Theorem 7.9]{AP_23} show that \(X_{\Sigma_M}\) and \(X_{\Sigma_0}\) can be connected by a finite sequence of toric blowups and blowdowns (weak factorization for tropical fans).
224 Therefore, the sequence \(\Sigma_m \to \Sigma_{m-1} \to \dotsb \to \Sigma_0\) in \eqref{eq_1} provides a concrete example of this theorem for matroids.
225 Furthermore, it shows that \(X_{\Sigma_M}\) can be obtained from \(X_{\Sigma_0}\) by a finite sequence of blowups.
226 \end{remark}
